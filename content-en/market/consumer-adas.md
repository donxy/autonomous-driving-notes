> [!info] Scope of this page
> First, draw the boundary: everything on this page is **L2** — navigation assistance features sold to car owners, with the driver always fully responsible. It and the L4 robotaxis in other articles on this site are two species, two markets, and two regulatory regimes. Penetration-rate figures are industry-institution data (definitions differ and are noted item by item); fitment volumes are mostly company claims. Information as of 2026-11-11.

## Penetration curve: city NOA and lidar falling below 200 USD

- CAAM's *2025 City NOA Automotive Assisted Driving Research Report* (released in January 2026): from January to November 2025, city NOA (Navigate on Autopilot) passenger car sales were **3.129 million vehicles**, accounting for **15.1%** of insurance registrations, up 5.6 percentage points from full-year 2024; of these, models under 300,000 yuan accounted for 68.9%, and automaker in-house development accounted for 78.3%. From the same report: in the first three quarters of 2025, the penetration rate of L2 combined driver assistance in new cars was 64%.
- Another institution's definition differs: Gasgoo counted city NOA fitments from January to October 2025 at 2.392 million vehicles, with a penetration rate of 12.8% — it also counts "pre-installed hardware", so it cannot be mixed with CAAM's sales definition. Both figures are true; they do not answer the same question.
- The feeding relationship on the hardware side: Hesai delivered 1.62 million lidars in 2025 (up +223% year on year), and its full-year average price fell from about 530 USD in 2024 to about **260 USD**, with the mainstay ATX priced below 200 USD. Lidar's shift from a "luxury-car option" to "standard fitment on 100,000-yuan-class cars" is the precondition for city NOA to move downmarket.

Source: [CAAM: 2025 City NOA Research Report (2026-01-14)](http://www.caam.org.cn/chn/3/cate_16/con_5237001.html)

## BYD's shout: God's Eye and "smart driving for all" (February 2025)

- **On 2025-02-10**, BYD released the "God's Eye" matrix (three tiers A/B/C) and its "smart driving for all" strategy: the first batch of 21 models added the features with no price increase, with full standard fitment on models above 100,000 yuan and fitment on most models below 100,000 yuan, bringing the lowest fitment price down to the 70,000-yuan class (Seagull).
- A functional footnote that must go with it: the lowest tier, God's Eye C, at the time focused on **highway navigation**, not city NOA — "70,000-yuan-class high-end smart driving" and "70,000-yuan-class city navigation" are two different things, and this is the most common substitution in this round of price cuts.
- Geely followed three weeks later: **on 2025-03-03** it released its unified solution "Qianli Haohan" (five tiers H1–H9, with minimum standard compute starting at 100 TOPS), promoting "equal rights in smart driving". Thereafter "high-end smart driving" changed from a selling point of 200,000-yuan-class models into an entry ticket across all price bands.

Sources: [EE Times China: God's Eye launch (2025-02)](https://www.eet-china.com/mp/a381229.html); [China Financial Information Network: Qianli Haohan (2025-03)](https://www.cnfin.com/gs-lb/detail/20250304/4195698_1.html)

## Huawei Qiankun: do not conflate the two modes

- **On 2024-04-24** it released its intelligent automotive solutions brand "Qiankun", and released Qiankun ADS 3.0 on the same day; in April 2025 it released ADS 4.0 (WEWA architecture), and officials say ADS has maintained one generation per year since 2024.
- Distinguishing the modes is the precondition for understanding Huawei's automotive business: **HI mode** — automakers adopt the Qiankun full-stack solution, and Huawei does not touch brand or channels (Voyah, Avatr, Deepal, etc.); **Harmony Intelligent Mobility Alliance** — Huawei deeply participates in product definition and sales (AITO, Luxeed, Stelato, Maextro); there is also flexible parts-level cooperation. Writing the two together as "Huawei builds cars" is one of the most frequent errors in Chinese reporting.
- Scale【Company claim】: In August 2025 Huawei announced that Qiankun smart driving fitments had exceeded **1 million vehicles** (11 brands, 28 models).

Source: [Cailian Press: ADS 4 launch (2025-04-22)](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2011795)

## Third-party duo: Momenta's flywheel and Zhuoyu's 7,000 yuan

- **Momenta**: Founded in Beijing in 2016 by Cao Xudong, positioned as "one flywheel (driven by mass-production data) + two legs (mass-production smart driving + fully driverless)". Its automaker shareholder group is its most distinctive asset: Mercedes-Benz led the B1 round in 2017, GM invested 300 million USD in 2021, SAIC is its largest institutional investor, and Toyota, Bosch, etc. are also on the shareholder list — **BYD is not its financial investor**; the two parties formed a joint venture in 2021, "Dipai Zhixing", and the two are often confused. By company account (relayed from prospectus materials): as of the end of 2025, cumulative design wins were 170 models, 68 in mass production, and more than 680,000 vehicles fitted; under third-party statistics, its third-party city NOA market share is about 64.5% (industry data).
- **Zhuoyu (formerly DJI Automotive)**: Started inside DJI in 2016, spun off independently in 2023, and formally adopted the "Zhuoyu" brand in June 2024. Its core product, the "Chengxing Platform", is based on inertial-navigation binocular vision; the basic version, 7V + 32 TOPS, targets extreme low cost — in 2024 the company publicly said about **7,000 yuan could put smart driving on a car**【Company claim】, and Wuling Baojun's 100,000-yuan-class models are its representative work; partner automakers include Volkswagen, SAIC-GM-Wuling, Chery, FAW, etc.

Sources: [Momenta official history](https://www.momenta.cn/article/19.html); [EE Times China: Zhuoyu Chengxing Platform](https://www.eet-china.com/mp/a440368.html)

## After 3·29: the great terminology renaming (2025)

- **On the evening of 2025-03-29**, a Xiaomi SU7 standard version in NOA state crashed in a construction diversion section of the Deshang Expressway in Anhui: after the driver took over, it still hit a concrete pile on the isolation belt at about 97 km/h and the vehicle caught fire, **killing 3 occupants**. Xiaomi published a summary of driving data on April 1; accident liability determination follows the official investigation, and this site makes no judgement. But its industry consequences were immediate.
- The preceding document was in fact already in place: a February 2025 notice from MIIT and the State Administration for Market Regulation already required combined driver assistance and OTA information to be included in product-access parameters, and OTA to be tested, verified, and filed.
- **On 2025-04-16**, MIIT held a meeting on access and OTA management (nearly 60 automakers attended, relayed from meeting minutes): in publicity, terms such as "autonomous driving, self-driving, smart driving, intelligent driving, high-end smart driving" were banned, to be uniformly called "(combined) driver assistance"; functions must not be declared under names such as valet parking or one-click summoning, and descriptions such as "hands-off, eyes-off" were banned; unverified OTA must not be pushed, and emergency OTA is handled as recall/production suspension.
- Result on the ground: within weeks, automaker order pages and publicity copy collectively changed "smart driving" to "assisted driving"; the mandatory national standard GB 47955—2026 *Safety Requirements for Combined Driver Assistance Systems of Intelligent Connected Vehicles* was subsequently released, setting the first mandatory standard for L2 (effective from 2027-01-01, according to industry reporting).
- A definition correction: saying "the word 'smart driving' was legally banned in 2025" is not accurate — this is terminology regulation at the access and publicity-management level, and "combined driver assistance" is a formal term that already existed in the national standard (GB/T 40429), merely pushed from standard documents to the consumer front stage.

Sources: [China Youth Daily: 3·29 accident report (2025-04)](https://zqb.cyol.com/pc/content/202504/03/content_409329.html); [CleanTechnica: China bans deceptive autonomous driving claims (2025-04-18)](https://cleantechnica.com/2025/04/18/china-bans-deceptive-autonomous-driving-claims)

## Control sample: Tesla FSD's three-year tug-of-war entering China

- On 2025-02-25, Tesla pushed city-road assistance features in batches to HW4 owners who had paid 64,000 yuan, initially still under the FSD name; immediately afterwards, due to the requirement that OTA be reviewed before pushing, a one-month free trial was suspended days after it started; on March 26 the official website renamed "FSD Intelligent Assisted Driving" to "Intelligent Assisted Driving", and the Autopilot package simultaneously removed the word "automatic" — the same renaming action as domestic automakers.
- Repeated delays followed: at the end of 2025 company executives said supervised FSD had obtained "partial approval" and was expected to land fully in early 2026, which did not materialise; as of November 2026, there is still no official confirmation of a full rollout. On one side are approval and data compliance, on the other naming and liability definitions — its progress in China is itself a footnote to this page's theme.

## The relationship between this page and L4

Consumer smart driving is not a "low-spec version" of robotaxis: its product is a feature, its revenue comes from car sales and subscriptions, liability rests with the driver, and regulation follows product access; while a robotaxi's product is transport capacity, its revenue comes from operations, liability rests with the operator, and regulation follows operating permits. The two lines share advances in perception and planning/control in technology, but diverge completely in business and liability — which is also why, when the same company (such as Baidu or Momenta) bets on both lines at once, it must run them with completely different organisations.
