## Founded in Silicon Valley, landed in Guangzhou (2017–2018)

- Founded in Silicon Valley in **April 2017** (originally named JingChi), founder Tony Han had previously been chief scientist of Baidu's Autonomous Driving Business Unit — from the same Baidu lineage as Pony.ai's James Peng and Tiancheng Lou. The company subsequently moved its headquarters to Guangzhou.
- In September 2017 it completed a Pre-A round of USD 52 million (led by Qiming Venture Partners, with NVIDIA participating).
- In **October 2018**, Alliance Ventures, the Renault-Nissan-Mitsubishi Alliance's fund, led the Series A round (tens of millions of USD; the exact amount was not disclosed), the Alliance's first investment in China; the company was renamed WeRide at the same time.

Source: [Alliance Ventures investment announcement](https://alliancernm.com/en/2022/01/26/alliance-ventures-leads-strategic-investment-in-weride-ai)

## The Guangzhou showroom (2019–2025)

- In June 2019 it obtained Guangzhou's first batch of 20 autonomous driving road-testing licences; in August it formed the operations joint venture "WeRide Go" with Baiyun Taxi and others.
- On **November 28, 2019**, it launched a **fully open** robotaxi trial operation for the public in Huangpu District, Guangzhou: covering 144.65 sq km, with no application or review required and ride-hailing directly via the App; the Guangzhou Municipal Government and media called it "China's first fully open robotaxi operation service". Two qualifiers must be retained: at the time it was a trial operation **with a safety driver**; Baidu also had a public trial operation in Changsha at the same time — whether "first" holds depends on the qualifier "fully open". The trial operation completed 8,396 orders in its first month.
- In July 2020 it obtained a remote testing permit for intelligent connected vehicles, which the company calls China's first 【Company claim】, and began fully driverless road testing; at the end of 2023 it opened China's first paid autonomous minibus route payable with a Yang Cheng Tong card on Guangzhou Bio Island.
- In 2025: in May it opened 8 round-the-clock demonstration operation routes in central Guangzhou; in September the GXR began 24-hour fully driverless commercial operations in Huangpu 【Company claim】. In Beijing, in February 2025 the GXR was approved to provide "in-vehicle driverless" paid service in the Economic-Technological Development Area (media reports).

Source: [Guangzhou Municipal Government: fully open operations in 2019](https://www.gz.gov.cn/ysgz/xwdt/ysdt/content/post_6992393.html)

## One platform, five vehicle types

WeRide's product matrix is the broadest among its peers, all based on the WeRide One universal platform:

- **Robotaxi**: the mass-production model GXR (released in October 2024)
- **Robobus**: autonomous minibus (used on the paid route on Guangzhou Bio Island and in shuttle pilots in France and Belgium)
- **Robovan**: driverless freight vehicle
- **Robosweeper**: driverless street-sweeping vehicle
- **ADAS advanced smart driving**: a solution for automakers' mass-production models, in cooperation with Bosch

This contrasts with peers that do robotaxi only: five product lines spread the R&D cost of the same technology base, but the commercialisation progress of each line is uneven — financially, robotaxi still accounts for only about 20% of revenue (see the ledger at the end).

Source: [WeRide product page](https://www.weride.ai/zh/products)

## Overseas methodology: the ladder approach in the UAE

- Robotaxi operations in Abu Dhabi since 2021. **In 2023 it obtained the UAE's first "national-level" autonomous driving licence** — covering all types of autonomous vehicles and allowing testing and operations on public roads nationwide, but specific operations still require approval at the level of each emirate; "national-level" does not mean "unconditional operation nationwide", which is the most common misreading.
- In **December 2024** it launched robotaxi service with Uber in Abu Dhabi (with an in-vehicle specialist at the time); in **October 2025** it obtained a "city-level fully driverless commercial permit", and in November it launched the Middle East's first L4 fully driverless commercial operations with Uber on Yas Island. It climbed the three steps — licence, platform, operations — one by one.
- Dubai: in September 2025 it obtained an RTA trial-operation permit and signed a cooperation MoU; the first 50 GXR vehicles are in trial operation with a safety driver, and the company's target is fully driverless commercialisation in 2026 — note this is still a trial-operation permit, not an operating permit.
- Other overseas locations (only those with sources listed): Singapore (testing with Grab in Punggol in September 2025), Switzerland (in November 2025 it obtained a driverless permit from the Federal Roads Office, Switzerland's first driverless permit for passenger-carrying robotaxis, targeting fully driverless public service in the first half of 2026), Belgium (Robobus in Leuven, a federal-level L4 testing licence), France (an L4 minibus pilot with Renault and others in the Drôme department in February 2025). On a company basis: its products hold licences in 8 countries in total.
- Expanding the Middle East fleet to 500–1,000 vehicles by the end of 2026 is a company **target**, not the current state.

Source: [Uber × WeRide: joint release on fully driverless commercial operations in Abu Dhabi (2025)](https://investor.uber.com/news-events/news/press-release-details/2025/WeRide-and-Uber-Launch-Middle-Easts-First-Fully-Driverless-Robotaxi-Commercial-Operations-in-Abu-Dhabi-UAE/default.aspx)

## Two listings: Nasdaq and HKEX

- **Listed on Nasdaq on October 25, 2024** (ticker WRD): 7.74 million ADSs were issued, priced at USD 15.5 (the bottom of the range), with the public offering raising about **USD 120 million**; there was also a concurrent private placement of about USD 320 million — the two claims of "IPO proceeds of USD 440 million" and "only USD 120 million" coexist in the media, and the difference is the private placement, so state the basis when citing; if the green shoe is exercised in full, the combined ceiling is about USD 458.5 million (conditional wording in the pricing release); this site uses about USD 440 million excluding the green shoe as the combined basis. The listing valuation was about USD 4.2–4.5 billion. The company calls itself "the world's first listed robotaxi company" (promotional basis); its listing was one month earlier than Pony.ai's (Chinese reporting often reverses the order).
- **Dual primary listing on HKEX on November 6, 2025** (code 0800): the issue price was 27.1 HKD, with proceeds of about 2.39 billion HKD (before the green shoe); the first-day close fell by about 10%.

Source: [Nasdaq: WeRide listing announcement (2024-10-25)](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/worlds-first-ipo-universal-autonomous-driving-technology-company-weride-officially)

## The ledger

- Q3 2025 (financial report): total revenue was 171 million yuan (up +144% year on year), of which robotaxi revenue was 35.3 million yuan (up +761% year on year), accounting for 20.7%; the gross margin was 32.9%; the net loss was 307 million yuan, narrowing by about 70% year on year.
- As of October 31, 2025: the global fleet exceeded 1,600 vehicles, of which nearly 750 were robotaxis; testing or operations in more than 30 cities in 11 countries.
- How to read this: revenue growth is fast and the narrowing loss is real, but robotaxi accounts for only 20% of revenue — this company's revenue structure currently looks more like a "multi-product-line autonomous driving supplier" than a pure robotaxi operator. For UE and cost bases see [[market/business-model|Business Model]].

Source: [WeRide 2025 Q3 financial report](https://files.quartr.com/reports/d9446-2025-11-24-10-55-46.pdf)
