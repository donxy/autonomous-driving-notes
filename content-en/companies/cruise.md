> [!info] About this page
> This is the most complete parabola of the first wave of autonomous driving: its valuation at its highest reached USD 30 billion, and in the end it was shut down by its parent company itself. Key figures such as the acquisition price, valuation and fines are each given with their source and basis; popular claims such as "GM spent USD 1 billion to buy Cruise" are corrected separately at the end of the page. Information as of 2026-11-11.

## A 40-person small company and the "USD 1 billion" rumour (2013–2016)

- Cruise Automation was founded in San Francisco in October 2013 by Kyle Vogt and Dan Kan, out of the Y Combinator winter 2014 batch; it initially made highway-driving retrofit kits, then turned to full-stack autonomous driving.
- On 2016-03-11, General Motors (GM) announced the acquisition of Cruise, when the company had approx. 40 people. **GM never disclosed the acquisition price in its announcement** — "more than USD 1 billion" was media reporting by Fortune/Re/code at the time, with no primary document to support it; the account calculated from SEC filings is between USD 580 million and USD 660 million (see the list of misreports at the end).

Sources: [TechCrunch: RIP Cruise (2024-12)](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/12/rip-cruise-robotaxi/); [GM acquisition announcement coverage](https://www.autoblog.com/news/gm-acquires-cruise-automation-official)

## Three years of capital: SoftBank, Honda, Microsoft (2018–2022)

- **May 2018**: the SoftBank Vision Fund invested USD 2.25 billion (in two tranches; after it was fully invested it would hold 19.6%), and GM simultaneously added USD 1.1 billion; Cruise was valued at USD 11.5 billion.
- **October 2018**: Honda announced a partnership to develop a dedicated autonomous vehicle, with an immediate equity investment of USD 750 million and approx. USD 2 billion to be invested over 12 years (a total commitment of USD 2.75 billion); Cruise's post-money valuation rose to USD 14.6 billion. Honda disclosed in 2024 that its actual total investment was USD 852 million — most of the commitment did not have time to be fulfilled.
- **2020-01-21**: it released the dedicated vehicle Cruise Origin, with no steering wheel and no pedals, which the company called "production ready"; but the vehicle did not comply with federal safety standards (FMVSS) and at the time could only be used in closed sites — mass production was ultimately never approved.
- **January 2021**: Microsoft led a round of more than USD 2 billion, and Cruise's valuation reached its peak of **USD 30 billion**.
- **March 2022**: GM bought out all of the equity held by SoftBank for USD 2.1 billion; SoftBank exited, and GM's holding rose to approx. 80%.

Sources: [GM Q2 2018 results materials](https://media.gm.com/content/dam/Media/gmcom/investor/2018/jul/GM-Q2-Press-Release-and-Highlights.pdf); [Honda partnership announcement](https://integration.honda.ca/newsdetails/nca/en/news/release/Honda-Joins-with-Cruise-and-General-Motors-to-Build-New-Autonomous-Vehicle); [GM announcement on buying out SoftBank (2022-03-18)](https://news.gm.com/home.detail.html/Pages/news/us/en/2022/mar/0318-cruise.html)

## The permit long march: from a testing licence to all-hours paid operation (2020–August 2023)

- In October 2020 it obtained a California DMV driverless **testing** permit; in September 2021 it obtained a driverless **deployment** permit (limited to a designated area in San Francisco, evening hours, a maximum of 30 mph).
- **On 2022-06-02**, the CPUC issued California's first driverless passenger deployment permit, allowing it to charge; on the evening of June 22 it began paid operation, which the company called the "first commercial driverless ride-hailing service in a major U.S. city".
- From the end of 2022 into 2023, the service expanded to Phoenix and Austin, and Houston, Dallas and Miami were in preparation.
- **On 2023-08-10**, the CPUC approved by a 3–1 vote for Cruise and Waymo to operate for pay **citywide and at all hours** in San Francisco — this was Cruise's highest point, less than two months from its collapse. Precursors had already appeared at the same time: on August 17 a Cruise vehicle collided with a fire truck, and the DMV required its active San Francisco fleet to be halved immediately.

Source: [CPUC announcement of the first deployment permit (2022-06-02)](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/news-and-updates/all-news/cpuc-issues-first-driverless-autonomous-vehicle-passenger-service-deployment-permit)

## 2023-10-02: twenty feet

- At approx. 21:30 that evening, at 5th and Market in San Francisco: a pedestrian in a crosswalk was struck by a **human-driven** Nissan, thrown into the adjacent lane, and landed in front of a driverless Cruise vehicle; the driver who caused the crash fled. **The first impact was not caused by Cruise** — this point is often written incorrectly.
- The Cruise vehicle braked hard but still ran over the pedestrian and stopped, with the pedestrian trapped under the vehicle; the vehicle then performed a "pull over" manoeuvre and, with a person under the vehicle, travelled another approx. 20 feet (approx. 6 metres) at up to approx. 7 mph before stopping completely; the pedestrian was dragged and seriously injured.
- The subsequent engineering analysis: the system misjudged the collision as a side impact, compounded by localisation error, and failed to recognise that there was a person under the vehicle; the post-collision handling logic chose to pull over rather than remain stationary in place. In November 2023, Cruise submitted a voluntary recall to NHTSA over this (23E-086), updating the collision-detection software on 950 vehicles.

Source: [Report on the California DMV suspension order (including the official reasons)](https://www.thedrive.com/news/california-suspends-cruise-robotaxi-operations-over-risk-to-public-safety)

## What fell was not the car, but trust (October–December 2023)

- On October 3, when it showed the crash video to regulators, playback stopped at the vehicle's first braking stop; it did not show the subsequent dragging segment, nor explain it verbally; the DMV learned of the dragging's existence from another agency, and only obtained the full video on October 13. The Quinn Emanuel investigation commissioned by Cruise (published in January 2024) concluded: the leadership did not intend to deceive, but failed in judgment and in its obsession with "correcting the media narrative" — while the leadership already knew about the dragging on the morning of October 3.
- **On 2023-10-24**, the DMV immediately suspended Cruise's driverless testing and deployment permits, on grounds including that the vehicles "pose an unreasonable risk to public safety" and that the company "misrepresented information related to the safety of the technology"; the CPUC followed the same day by suspending its charging authorisation. On October 26, Cruise announced the suspension of all driverless operations nationwide.
- **On 2023-11-19**, co-founder and CEO Kyle Vogt resigned; in December, 9 executives were fired, and full-time employees were cut by approx. 24% (approx. 900 people).
- The three penalties were all for "concealment" rather than the crash itself: a CPUC fine of **USD 112,500** (15 days without providing complete information; settled in June 2024); an NHTSA consent-order fine of **USD 1.5 million** (neither the 1-day nor the 10-day report on the crash disclosed the dragging; September 2024); and a U.S. Department of Justice deferred-prosecution agreement criminal fine of **USD 500,000** (admitting it submitted a false record with intent to influence a federal investigation; November 2024).

Sources: [DOJ announcement (2024-11-14)](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndca/pr/cruise-admits-submitting-false-report-influence-federal-investigation-and-agrees-pay); [CPUC settlement decision document](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Efile/G000/M534/K105/534105790.PDF)

## Endgame and the bill (2024–2025)

- In the first half of 2024 Cruise did genuinely try to restart: human-driven vehicles returned to road testing in Phoenix, GM injected another USD 850 million, and the leadership was changed; but the California permits were never restored. In July 2024, GM indefinitely suspended Origin mass production, and the next-generation vehicle switched to the Chevrolet Bolt platform.
- **On 2024-12-10, GM announced it would no longer fund Cruise's robotaxi business**, merging it into GM internally and turning to personal-car autonomous driving based on Super Cruise; GM expected to save more than USD 1 billion per year after the restructuring.
- The total bill (calculated by AP from the shareholder reports GM submitted to the SEC): since the 2016 acquisition, Cruise's cumulative operating losses were **more than USD 10 billion**, with revenue in the same period of less than USD 500 million; in 2023 alone, the pre-tax loss was approx. USD 2.7 billion. Barra's statement in December 2024 was: "On top of the USD 10 billion already invested, substantial incremental time and capital would still be needed."
- In February 2025, GM completed its full acquisition of Cruise, Cruise cut nearly another 50% of its workforce (approx. 1,000 people), and the remaining personnel (approx. 88% of them engineering staff) were merged into GM. As of 2026-11-11: Cruise as an independent brand and robotaxi service has ended, its California driverless permits were never restored; the legal entity is retained as a wholly owned GM subsidiary, and the three-year compliance period under the DOJ deferred prosecution is still running.

Sources: [GM strategic adjustment announcement (2024-12-10)](https://news.gm.com/home.detail.html/Pages/news/us/en/2024/dec/1210-gm.html); [TechCrunch: full acquisition and workforce cuts (2025-02-04)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/04/cruise-to-slash-workforce-by-50-after-gm-cuts-funding-to-robotaxi-operations/)

## Common misreports

1. **"GM spent USD 1 billion to buy Cruise"** — GM never disclosed the price; the SEC-filing account is approx. USD 580 million–USD 660 million, and "more than USD 1 billion" is a 2016 media report with no documentary basis.
2. **"A Cruise driverless car knocked down a pedestrian"** — the first impact came from a human hit-and-run driver; Cruise's responsibility lies in the post-collision dragging handling and the subsequent reporting, not in the first impact.
3. **"The USD 1.5 million fine was because of the dragging incident"** — the three fines (CPUC USD 112,500 / NHTSA USD 1.5 million / DOJ USD 500,000) all penalised concealment and false records in the reporting process, not the crash itself.
4. **"Honda invested USD 2.75 billion"** — that was the total commitment in 2018; Honda's actual total investment was USD 852 million.
5. **"GM shut Cruise immediately after the crash"** — there were 14 months in between: a restart attempt, another USD 850 million injection, and a CEO change all happened; "destined to be shut down" is a post-hoc simplification.
