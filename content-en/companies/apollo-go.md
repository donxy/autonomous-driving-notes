## Origins: the car brain and the "Android of autonomous driving" (2013–2017)

- Baidu's driverless vehicle project started in 2013, led by Baidu Research Institute, centred on the "Baidu Car Brain" (HD map, localization, perception, decision-making and control).
- In December 2015, a Baidu driverless vehicle completed a fully autonomous road test in mixed Beijing traffic (Jingxin Expressway — Fifth Ring Road — Olympic Park, with a top speed of 100 km/h), the first in China; the Autonomous Driving Business Unit was established in the same month.
- In March 2017 it was consolidated into the Intelligent Driving Group (IDG); on **April 19, 2017**, Lu Qi announced the Apollo plan at the Shanghai Auto Show: opening its autonomous driving software platform to the industry (four parts: vehicle, hardware, software, and cloud data services), dubbed the "Android of autonomous driving". This open-platform route later gave way to self-operated operations — Apollo Go is what Baidu autonomous driving ultimately grew into.

Source: [GlobeNewswire: Apollo plan announcement (2017-04-19)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2017/04/19/1018939/0/en/baidu-announces-project-apollo-opening-up-its-autonomous-driving-platform.html)

## From platform to operations (2019–2024)

- Trial operations came first: trial operations began in Changsha in September 2019 with 45 vehicles; Changsha opened in April 2020 and Cangzhou in August 2020; on **September 10, 2020** the service was launched in Beijing (Yizhuang, Haidian and Shunyi, about 40 vehicles and about 700 km of open roads, with a safety driver, registration required to ride); from October 10 of the same year it was fully opened with no reservation required (the free period ran until November 6; this milestone is attested only in secondary reporting).
- The "Apollo Go" brand and standalone App launched in 2021. Vehicle generations: the fifth-generation Apollo Moon (2021, based on the ARCFOX Alpha T and others; media reports put the per-vehicle cost at about 480,000 yuan) → the sixth-generation RT6 (first unveiled in July 2022 and delivered in May 2024 under the production name "Yichi 06", priced at 204,600 yuan; see the Business Model page).

Source: [Baidu official release: Apollo Go Beijing launch (PR Newswire, 2020-09-10)](https://en.prnasia.com/releases/global/baidu-launches-apollo-go-robotaxi-service-in-beijing-advancing-its-leading-position-in-autonomous-driving-291109.shtml)

## The Wuhan case: scale-up, controversy and a system failure

- On **August 8, 2022**, Wuhan and Chongqing were the first to issue fully driverless commercial pilot policies, and Baidu obtained one of the first driverless demonstration operation qualifications in China (no safety driver in the vehicle, paid operations). Wuhan thereafter became Apollo Go's largest operating city.
- The **July 2024** low-price controversy (see the Business Model page for details); in the same month the Wuhan Municipal Transport Bureau clarified that the actual deployment was **more than 400 vehicles** — "1,000 vehicles" was the within-year plan and first-batch delivery basis from Apollo Day 2024, misreported as the number already deployed; this site's homepage once specifically corrected this item.
- On the **evening of March 31, 2026**, a large number of Apollo Go vehicles in Wuhan stalled while driving (including on elevated roads and arterial roads), and some passengers were trapped for nearly two hours; Wuhan traffic police reported a preliminary assessment of a system failure, with no injuries, and service was suspended across Wuhan the following day. Permit and operations adjustments in multiple cities then continued for months; single-quarter orders in Q2 2026 fell sharply (see the next section) — on the Q2 earnings call Baidu said only that order volume was affected by "operations adjustments in some domestic cities due to regulatory factors", without naming the Wuhan incident; attributing the decline to the post-Wuhan-incident adjustments is this site's judgment based on the timeline — this was the largest systemic service interruption in China's robotaxi operating history, and this site will continue to track its investigation conclusions (as of 2026-11-19, Baidu has not published a full technical review).

Source: [CNR: Wuhan vehicle stall incident (2026-04-01)](https://news.cnr.cn/native/gd/kx/20260401/t20260401_527569590.shtml)

## The order curve: the numbers are real, the basis changed

All of the following are disclosed in Baidu's financial reports, but please understand the basis before reading the numbers:

| Time | Cumulative orders | Basis note |
|---|---|---|
| April 2024 | Over 6 million orders | Includes staffed orders from the safety-driver era |
| January 2025 | Over 9 million orders | Same as above; from February 2025, operations nationwide shifted to 100% fully driverless |
| August 2025 | Over 14 million orders | Fully driverless orders in Q2 alone exceeded 2.2 million orders |
| End of October 2025 | Over 17 million orders | Weekly order peak exceeded 250,000 【Company claim: Baidu】 |
| February 2026 | Over 20 million orders | Fully driverless orders of 3.4 million in Q4 2025 alone (Baidu Q4 2025 financial report, 2026-02-26) |
| End of June 2026 | Over 23 million orders | Covering "28 cities" (see footnote) |
| Q3 2026 | Not disclosed | The Q3 financial report (2026-11-16) gave no order count and reported mileage instead: autonomous driving mileage of about 34 million km in the quarter and a cumulative total of over 390 million km (as of 2026-10-31); global footprint of 30 cities (October 2026) |

- **Two padding points**: First, the cumulative figures before 2025 mix the staffed and driverless eras; "fully driverless orders in a single quarter" is a metric broken out separately only after 2025 — comparing 6 million directly with 23 million overstates the growth rate of fully driverless operations. Second, "28 cities" uses Baidu's "global footprint" basis, mixing paid operations, road testing and contracted preparations; Baidu does not publish a separate count of domestic operating cities, so it cannot be read as "28 cities in paid operation" (on the same basis, 30 cities had been reported by October 2026).
- A reverse footnote: fully driverless orders in Q2 2026 were about 1 million in the single quarter, a sharp fall from 3.2 million in Q1; on the earnings call management said order volume was temporarily affected by "operations adjustments in some domestic cities due to regulatory factors", that affected cities had gradually resumed from August, and did not name the Wuhan incident (earnings-call basis); this site judges the post-Wuhan-incident operations adjustments to be the main cause. The Q3 financial report no longer disclosed single-quarter fully driverless order numbers — this series breaks off here and mileage becomes the headline instead (see the table above); the only single-city basis was Shenzhen fully driverless orders up about +170% quarter on quarter (no absolute number).

Sources: [Baidu Q2 2026 financial report (SEC filing, 2026-08-18)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526394299/d152888dex991.pdf); [Baidu Q4 2025 financial report (SEC filing, 2026-02-26)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1329099/000119312526076505/d117608dex991.htm)

## Evidence levels for technology and safety claims

- Apollo ADFM (released in May 2024): Baidu calls it "the world's first autonomous driving foundation model supporting L4" and says its safety is "more than 10 times higher than human drivers" — both are company statements with no independent verification.
- Safety figures: in 2024 Baidu said its vehicles' actual insurance claim rate was only 1/14 that of human drivers; the Q2 2026 financial-report basis says an airbag deployment occurs once every 14.4 million km. Both are unilateral company data with no third-party audit, and are not in the same evidence tier as Waymo's Swiss Re claims study (a collaborative study) or Safety Impact Hub (self-assessed but with a published methodology); before comparing across companies, read [[regulation/safety-evidence|How to read safety data]].
- In November 2026, Baidu disclosed that its world model "Apollo World" has been used in testing and validation for Apollo Go 【Company claim】.

## Three routes overseas

- **Hong Kong (first right-hand-drive stop)**: on November 29, 2024 the Transport Department issued Hong Kong's first autonomous vehicle pilot licence (10 vehicles, testing in North Lantau, later expanded to 30 vehicles with multiple area expansions).
- **Middle East**: in Dubai, an MoU was signed with the RTA in March 2025 and the first testing licence was obtained in July; in January 2026 a fully driverless testing licence was obtained and the first overseas operations centre was opened, and in March fully driverless commercial operations began via its own App 【Unverified】 (the Q3 2026 financial report confirms that its fully driverless commercial operations expanded in area during Q3 and the fleet size doubled, but gives no start date); in Abu Dhabi, in cooperation with AutoGo under K2, a fully driverless commercial operation licence was obtained in November 2025, and fully driverless paid service launched on Yas Island in January 2026.
- **Europe**: the "AmiGo" cooperation with Swiss PostBus (announced in October 2025, four cantons in eastern Switzerland, up to 25 vehicles): mapping tests from December 2025 (manually driven, no passengers), autonomous driving (hands-off) tests from June 1, 2026 (a special permit from Switzerland's ASTRA has been obtained), targeting regular operations without a safety driver from 2027.
- **Going overseas via platforms**: multi-year cooperations with Uber in July 2025 and Lyft in August 2025 (see the Business Model page for details). The three parallel routes are the concrete form of its asset-light shift: self-operated (Hong Kong, Dubai), partnerships (Abu Dhabi, Switzerland), and platform distribution (Uber/Lyft).

## Open questions

- **Asset-light and UE**: since the Q4 2024 financial report Baidu has repeatedly emphasised a shift to asset-light expansion and said cities such as Wuhan have achieved per-vehicle UE break-even — all are management statements; see the Business Model page for the footnotes.
- **Spin-off rumours**: during 2025–2026 rumours that "Apollo Go will be spun off for independent financing / listing" have appeared from time to time; Baidu has never officially announced this, and this site does not treat them as credible or record them as fact.
- **Review of the Wuhan incident**: the technical cause of, and corrective conclusions for, the March 31, 2026 system failure are this company's largest current open matter.
