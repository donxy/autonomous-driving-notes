## 2009: Secret start

- The project started on **January 17, 2009** inside Google X, under the internal codename "Chauffeur", initiated by Sebastian Thrun and Anthony Levandowski.
- Its core members came directly from the winning teams of the [[history/darpa-challenge|DARPA Challenges]]: Chris Urmson (CMU's 2007 winning team), Mike Montemerlo (Stanford's 2005 winning team), and others. The Challenges' entry roster became this project's hiring list.

## 2010: Public disclosure

- On October 9, 2010, the project was disclosed publicly via reporting in The New York Times and Thrun's official Google blog; by then the test vehicles had already accumulated 140,000+ miles of driving.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Google Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Car); [IEEE Spectrum (2014)](https://spectrum.ieee.org/plate-and-switch-googles-selfdriving-car-is-a-transformer-too)

## 2012: The first license

- Nevada's autonomous driving regulations took effect on March 1, 2012; in May 2012, the Nevada DMV issued the first U.S. autonomous driving test license (AU-001), for a Toyota Prius modified by Google; a Lexus RX450h was also being tested in the same period.
- Source: [National Geographic (2012-05-09)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/google-gets-first-driverless-car-license-strings-attached)

## 2014–2017: The Firefly pod car

- The Firefly prototype was unveiled at the end of May 2014, and a functional version was completed in December 2014: **no steering wheel, no pedals**, positioned as a test platform rather than a production vehicle, and retired in 2017.
- On October 20, 2015, Steve Mahan, who is blind, rode alone in a Firefly on streets in a public residential area in Austin, Texas, completing the trip with no safety driver and no escort vehicle. The event itself is verified; Waymo calls it "the world's first fully driverless ride on public roads" — "world's first" is a **company publicity claim**.
- Source: [Waymo official blog (2023 retrospective)](https://waymo.com/blog/2023/03/expanding-waymos-testing-to-austin/)

## December 2016: Spin-out as Waymo

- Announced on **December 13, 2016**: the project spins out from Google X into **Waymo**, an "Other Bets" company under Alphabet (note: not the commonly cited "October 2016" account; this site's research has corrected it). The CEO was John Krafcik (he had already been hired as the project's CEO in August 2015).
- Source: [TechCrunch (2016-12-13)](https://techcrunch.com/2016/12/13/googles-self-driving-car-unit-spins-out-as-waymo/)

## 2018–2020: From project to service

- On December 5, 2018, **Waymo One** launched: a commercial autonomous ride-hailing service in the Phoenix metropolitan area (Chandler, Tempe, Mesa, Gilbert, about 100 square miles), with the first users being about 400 Early Rider members, a fleet of Chrysler Pacificas, and safety drivers still present at launch. Waymo calls it the "world's first commercial autonomous service" — "first" is a publicity claim, but it is generally recognized in the industry. Source: [AppleInsider (2018-12-05)](https://appleinsider.com/articles/18/12/05/waymo-one-launches-as-worlds-first-commercial-autonomous-taxi-service)
- On October 8, 2020, Waymo opened a **fully driverless** (no safety driver) service to the public in Phoenix. Source: [Waymo official blog (2020-10-08)](https://waymo.com/blog/2020/10/waymo-is-opening-its-fully-driverless-service-in-phoenix/)

## Why this line matters

- It turned the "multisensor fusion + HD map + limited area" route validated by the Challenges into an operable service — for its position on routes, see [[technology/routes|The Route Debate: Lidar vs. Vision-Only]].
- It is also one of the industry's sources of talent and methods: Uber ATG (which poached people from CMU to build its own team in 2015) and the autonomous driving departments of various automakers have many members from this lineage.
- The company's current status (order volume, cities, fleet, financing) is a separate note: [[companies/waymo|Waymo]].
