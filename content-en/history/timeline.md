## Origins (1986–2003)

- **1986** · The CMU Navlab project starts; in the same year Dickmanns' VaMoRs begins autonomous driving experiments —— [[history/darpa-challenge|DARPA Challenges]]
- **1995** · Navlab 5 "No Hands Across America": Pittsburgh→San Diego, with the steering wheel automated for 98.2% of the time —— [[history/darpa-challenge|DARPA Challenges]]

## The Challenge era (2004–2007)

- **2004** · The first DARPA Grand Challenge: no finisher, the farthest 7.4 miles —— [[history/darpa-challenge|DARPA Challenges]]
- **2005** · Stanford's Stanley wins, 5 teams finish
- **2007** · Urban Challenge: CMU's Boss wins in an urban environment; members of the winning team later became the backbone of the Google project

## Tech companies enter (2009–2016)

- **2009** · The Google driverless project starts secretly inside Google X —— [[history/google-to-waymo|From the Google Project to Waymo]]
- **2012** · Nevada issues the first U.S. autonomous driving test license (for Google's Prius)
- **2014** · Tesla launches Autopilot hardware HW1 (Mobileye platform); Google unveils the Firefly prototype with no steering wheel
- **2015** · Tesla Autopilot software goes live with v7.0 (L2, the driver bears full responsibility); Uber founds ATG and poaches about 40 researchers from CMU; the predecessor of Baidu's Apollo Go begins laying out road testing
- **2016-12-13** · The Google project spins out as **Waymo** (date corrected by this site's research, not October); in the same year Tesla releases HW2 and claims it has "the hardware needed for full self-driving" (publicity claim, never delivered in unsupervised form); GM acquires Cruise (price not officially disclosed)

## First wave and pullback (2017–2020)

- **2017** · Baidu releases the Apollo open platform
- **2018-03-18** · Uber ATG strikes and kills pedestrian Elaine Herzberg in Tempe, Arizona — the first pedestrian killed by an autonomous vehicle; the NTSB report found the safety driver's distraction to be the direct cause and Uber's safety culture to be a contributing factor —— [[history/shakeout|Industry Shakeout: Failures and Exits]]
- **2018-12** · Waymo One launches in Phoenix, the first commercial autonomous ride-hailing service (with safety drivers at first)
- **2020-09** · Baidu Apollo Go launches in Beijing (09-10, registration required to ride) —— [[companies/apollo-go|Baidu Apollo Go]]
- **2020-10** · Waymo opens a fully driverless service to the public in Phoenix; Tesla FSD Beta is first released to a small group (L2)

## Shakeout (2022–2024)

- **2022-06** · Cruise obtains the California CPUC's first driverless passenger deployment permit (issued 06-02) and starts charging in the same month (officially announced on 06-23 that charging had begun "starting last night") —— [[companies/cruise|Cruise: From Peak Valuation to Shutdown]]
- **2022-10** · Argo AI shuts down (Ford and Volkswagen withdraw funding) —— [[history/shakeout|Industry Shakeout: Failures and Exits]]
- **2023-10** · The Cruise San Francisco dragging incident: after being struck, a pedestrian was dragged about 20 feet; the California DMV suspends its testing and deployment permits, and Cruise suspends driverless operations nationwide —— [[companies/cruise|Cruise: From Peak Valuation to Shutdown]]
- **2024-03** · Tesla renames FSD Beta to **FSD (Supervised)**; its official characterization has always been L2
- **2024-10** · Tesla unveils the Cybercab with no steering wheel; Waymo completes a $5.6 billion financing round; in the same month Alphabet discloses at its earnings call that its weekly paid rides exceed 150,000
- **2024-11/12** · Pony.ai and WeRide list in succession —— [[companies/pony-ai|Pony.ai]] · [[companies/weride|WeRide]]; Apollo Go obtains its first pilot license in Hong Kong —— [[companies/apollo-go|Baidu Apollo Go]]; **on 2024-12-10 GM ends Cruise's robotaxi business** — the emblematic exit of the first wave

## Scaling and L3 deployment (2025–2026)

- **2025-04** · Waymo's weekly paid rides reach 250,000 (disclosed in Alphabet earnings); Aurora begins driverless truck commercial operations between Dallas and Houston — the first paid driverless operation on public roads on the trucking line, arriving about ten years late —— [[market/trucking|Autonomous Trucks: The Long-Haul Trucking Line]]
- **2025-06-22** · Tesla launches its robotaxi pilot in Austin: no one in the driver's seat, but a safety monitor sits in the front passenger seat — not unsupervised operation —— [[companies/tesla|Tesla]]
- **2025-07** · Baidu reaches a multi-year partnership with Uber and partners with Lyft in August, accelerating its overseas expansion —— [[market/robotaxi-china|China's Robotaxi Landscape]]
- **2025-11** · Waymo opens driverless highway service to paying passengers; Pony.ai and WeRide dual-list on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange
- **2025-12-15** · **China's first L3 admissions land**: MIIT conditionally permits the Changan Deepal SL03 and the Arcfox Alpha S6, with designated user entities to carry out on-road pilot use on limited road sections —— [[regulation/levels-and-rules|Autonomous Driving Levels and Regulation]]
- **2026-02** · Waymo completes a $16 billion financing round, at a post-money valuation of about $126 billion; Apollo Go's cumulative orders break through 20,000,000【Company claim】
- **2026-03** · Waymo's weekly paid rides reach 500,000 (Alphabet disclosure); NHTSA upgrades its investigation into FSD in low-visibility scenarios to an engineering analysis
- **2026-03-31** · A large-scale Apollo Go system failure in Wuhan: large numbers of vehicles stall while driving, service is suspended across Wuhan the next day, and operations are adjusted in multiple cities over the following months —— [[companies/apollo-go|Baidu Apollo Go]]
- **2026-06-24** · UNECE adopts the first global technical regulation (GTR) for ADS — "no unreasonable risk" and the safety case become global text —— [[regulation/us-eu-rules|Regulation in the U.S. and EU]]
- **2026-09** · Waymo's paid metropolitan areas increase to 15; the Cybercab joins the paid fleet in Austin (company claims and regulatory audits proceed in parallel, see [[companies/tesla|Tesla]])
- **2026-10** · China's MIIT confirms that the second batch of L3 admission pilots is "under way", with the list not yet published —— [[regulation/levels-and-rules|Autonomous Driving Levels and Regulation]]

## How to read this timeline

Three lines run at the same time: the **technology line** (Challenges → modular pipeline → end-to-end, see [[technology/stack|Technology Stack at a Glance]]), the **business line** (the scaling of Waymo and Chinese robotaxis vs. the exits of Cruise/Argo), and the **regulation line** (from test licenses to L3 admission, see [[regulation/levels-and-rules|Autonomous Driving Levels and Regulation]]). Which line accelerates in which year basically determines how hot the industry was that year.
